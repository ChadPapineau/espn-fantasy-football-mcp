## Bench and IR — the template, the IR rules, the hidden-bench play

**The derived bench template.** With five bench spots in a ten-team league the replacement curves usually say: one K and one D/ST in their starting slots and none on the bench (both stream), no backup QB or TE unless that position's streamability is low, and the five spots spent on running back and receiver depth — the steep running-back curve makes injury cover worth more there. The tool derives the template from this league's own curves (`bench_template.basis`) and shows it as derived; the user's bench is compared with it, never forced into it.

**Handcuffs and stashes, per case.** A handcuff is worth its expected points if the starter misses time times the chance he does, against the best free agent; with five bench spots at most one handcuff usually has positive value, and a handcuff to someone else's starter is a stash. A stash (an injured or suspended player expected back) is worth his value from his return week — through the fantasy playoffs, not the season's end. Both are valued per case, never by a rule.

**The IR rules ESPN enforces.** An IR slot may hold only a player whose status is `OUT` or `INJURY_RESERVE`. When a player in IR becomes healthy (any other status), the roster is invalid: every add — claims and free-agent pickups — is blocked until he is activated or dropped, and activating him needs an open bench spot, so a full bench forces a drop first. An `OUT` or `INJURY_RESERVE` player on the bench can move to an open IR slot and free a bench spot. Activate players after the waiver run, never the night before a pending claim: the claim fails on an invalid roster.

**The hidden-bench play.** A player who went into IR while eligible and is now `QUESTIONABLE` may stay in the slot while that tag lasts — in effect a sixth bench spot. Its three risks: the tag can clear at any moment, making the roster invalid and blocking adds until a move is made; the move needs a bench spot, so one bench player must be designated as the drop in advance; and the activation can collide with a pending claim or a lineup lock. The tool names the three risks and the designated drop whenever the play is available.

**Droppables and adds.** A drop is priced by the player's rest-of-season value and the risk a rival adds him back (`re_add_risk`); a player ESPN marks undroppable is never named. In a league with an acquisition limit the adds remaining are part of the plan.
