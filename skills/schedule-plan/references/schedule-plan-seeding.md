## The seeding race — two readings, marginal values, byes

**Two readings of "seeded by points".** Under ESPN's rule ("Playoff Seeding Tie Breaker: Total Points For") the seeds go by record and points for only breaks ties. Under the other reading the commissioner qualifies and seeds by points for alone — ESPN has no field for it, so the server reads last season's seeds (`seeding_evidence`: did the seeds follow the records or the points?) or the owner's answer recorded by `onboard`. The two readings can disagree about the same team: a 3–1 team sixth in points for is comfortable under ESPN's rule and on the bubble when points alone count. When the reading is unconfirmed and the evidence does not settle it, both are simulated (`seeding_mode: "both"`) and shown side by side — never one asserted.

**The marginal-values table.** The season simulator re-runs the remaining season from perturbed states: one more win, 20 / 40 / 80 more points for, a lineup whose spread is 0.7× or 1.4× today's. Each row is the change in playoff probability and first-round-bye probability. Reading it: if one win is worth +0.12 playoff probability and 40 points are worth +0.06, a win is worth about 80 points to this team right now — `pf_per_win`, the exchange rate `start-sit` uses when points for is the tiebreak. Under points-only seeding wins are worth nothing and the exchange rate is null.

**Variance is a position.** A team comfortably above the cut line gains from a steadier lineup; a team below it gains from a higher-ceiling one. The `σ×0.7` and `σ×1.4` rows say which this team is, in probability — never as a temperament.

**Byes and holes.** A bye week costs what the replacement for each hole scores below the starter, not the number of players out. A cluster in a week the team is likely to be eliminated by matters little (`weight.p_alive`); a cluster in the last regular-season weeks, with the race tight, matters most. The fixes (`fixes[]`) are priced — a free-agent stream for the week, a bench spot rebalanced, a trade — each with its deadline.

**Playoff weeks.** The fantasy playoffs are the weeks in `matchup_periods` after the regular season, one round a week; the top seeds may sit out the first round. Playoff-week opponents' defensive strength this far out is close to noise, so the matchup multipliers are shrunk hard (`shrink_w`), and some NFL teams rest starters in week 17 — a flagged risk, never a rule.
