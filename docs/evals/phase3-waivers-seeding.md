## C4 — seeding reproduction

**Code:** `src/domain/analytics/seeding.ts` `rankFinalStandings` (additive: the simulator's own
`tiebreakChain` + `rankAll` applied once to a finished season) and
`src/domain/analytics/seeding-reproduce/` (`reproduceSeason`, `summarizeReproductions`,
`reproductionInputFrom`). **Tests:** `tests/domain/analytics/seeding-reproduce/` — `history.test.ts`
(the recorded seasons, hard), `reproduce.test.ts` (every tiebreak level on constructed seasons,
every finding, and fast-check properties against an independent implementation of research 05 §2.1's
chain written in `helpers.ts`), `rank-final.test.ts`.

**Method.** Each previous season is read the way the product reads one: the provider in fixture
mode over `fixtures/espn/recorded/history` (`getStandings`, `getMatchups`), and the provider's own
normalisers over the recorded `mSettings` (`normalizeLeagueRules`, `normalizeLeague` for
`isPlayoffMatchupEdited` — the provider's settings read asks for `mSettings` + `mNav` together and
the history recorded `mSettings` alone). The ranking reads ESPN's own final standings (what ESPN
seeds from); the regular-season rows (period ≤ `matchupPeriodCount`; byes and playoff rows not read)
give head-to-head and division records and are recomputed into records that must agree with the
standings to 0.01 points (`records_disagree` otherwise). Reading (a) = division winners first when
the league has more than one division, then `TOTAL_POINTS_SCORED`'s win % → PF → head-to-head →
division record → points against → coin; reading (b) = PF → coin. **Exact** means every team's seed
equals its position with no coin flip needed. A coin-flip tie is detected (ranking under both coin
orders) and reported, with whether ESPN's seeds follow the chain everywhere above it. A season
neither reading reproduces must carry an explaining finding — a bracket edit, records that
disagree, incomplete seeds, an unfinished season, an unknown rule, a coin flip with the seeds
consistent above it; a bare seed mismatch is **unexplained and fails C4**.

## C5 — the fitted waiver DP

**Code:** `src/domain/analytics/waiver-fit/` — `fitSurplusDistribution` (+ `surplusRate`,
`surplusObservationsFromRuns`), `fitDrift` (+ `driftFromClaimRuns`, `driftFromAcquisitions`),
`fitLeaguePremium`, `comparePremium`, `calibrateClearsToFa`, `clearProbability`, `fitDemandScale`,
and `fitPremiumFromFeed` — the one call for the E5 tool: persisted transactions (drift from the
processed claims, each run's week from ESPN's `scoring_period`), else the standings' acquisition
totals (the bound), plus the logged E5 runs (the surplus);
`src/domain/analytics/waiverDp.ts` gains `rates` (the DP solved from any rate distribution,
validated by `isValidRateDistribution`) and a bounded table cache; `src/domain/analytics/waivers.ts`
gains `premium_fit` — E5 reports `premium_basis: "league_fitted"` and reads Π and its band from the
fitted tables at its own `(k, W)` when the fit is `league_fitted` and was solved for this league
size (the P1 engine passes it through). **Tests:** `tests/domain/analytics/waiver-fit/` (unit and
properties), `tests/backtest/waivers-fitted.test.ts` (the replay, the drift evidence),
`tests/backtest/waivers-clears.test.ts` (the calibration).

**The fit.** One observation per processing run: the best claimable player's surplus over the drop
candidate as a per-week rate, r = s / (W + 1) (the claim's value spans W + 1 weeks, changelog R5-1).
Observations are split linearly onto the support {0, 1, 2.5, 4.5, 7, 10, 14, 20} (the mean is kept;
above 20 clamped and counted) and combined with the cold-start table as a Dirichlet prior worth 8
runs. The basis becomes `league_fitted` at 13 runs (one regular season of claim weeks); before that
E5 keeps the cold-start table and says why (ADV OBJ-03). Drift c from the league's claim feed is a
Beta posterior (prior 0.25 worth 40 team-weeks, ≥ 30 team-weeks to move); from acquisition totals
it is only an **upper bound** (free-agent adds count there too), so c stays 0.25 unless the bound
is lower — never set to the bound itself when the bound is higher. The band stays plan 07 E5's
×0.5 / ×1.5 surplus re-solve; with demand reading the base rate the DP is homogeneous in the
surplus, so the band is exactly 0.5 Π and 1.5 Π (a property test holds it).

**Reading the replay.** The bottleneck the first build named was the forecast, not the premium: on
the A9a harness's trailing-4 points per game the rule lost to trending per successful claim in 2024
(−32.6 %) and tied it in 2025 (−1.8 %), and replacing the cold-start Π with the fitted one moved the
rule's per-claim surplus by under 2 % — the strike decides *whether* to claim, the forecast decides
*whom*, and a trailing-4 rate reacts to a role change a week or more after "most points last week"
does. The B2b fix swaps in the Phase 3 opportunity forecast (`tests/backtest/helpers/v2-forecast.ts`:
`v2-opportunity`'s expected points if active for the candidate's next game — EWM shares with change
points, shrinkage per rate, location-based TDs, the market line, the regressed matchup — read as of
the claim week from the committed C1 excerpt, over replacement, held with the same P(role holds)
prior); the fit, the premium, the strike and the harness are unchanged. On it the fitted rule beats
trending per successful claim by +18.2 % (2024) and +66.7 % (2025), and realises more surplus per
season in 2024 (198.6 vs 174.8) though not in 2025 (131.0 vs 140.0: the 2024-fitted premium is
richer than 2025's wire, so the rule claims less — 4.8 successful claims against trending's 8.6; the
cold-start table, which claims more, realised 199.3). The forecast also feeds the rivals' demand in
the harness, so trending's own numbers move with it (common random numbers hold within a
forecaster). Caveats: the replay league has no ESPN projections for free agents, so v2's own mean
stands in for E1's (which anchors on ESPN's at `weight_espn` 1.0); 1,158 candidate-weeks the C1
excerpt does not cover keep the trailing-4 forecast (counted in the table's lead-in). The diagnostic
`trending_strike` almost never fires on v2's forecast (0.2 and 0 claims a season): the trending

**Reading the calibration.** E5's cold-start demand gives every rival a 5 % chance of claiming even
a worthless player (q(0) = 0.05), so with nine rivals it predicts a dropped bench player clears only
63 % of the time; in `league-b` 94–96 % cleared the next week. A pure multiplier on q0 + q1·r
(λ ≈ 0.05–0.07, at or near its bound) cannot fit both the near-zero demand for dropped players and
the demand for real targets, so calibration-in-the-large came right while 2024's worst decile stayed
13.2 points off. The B2b fix fits the intercept and the slope separately (`fitDemandCurve`, maximum
likelihood, held out on the other season): q0 ≈ 0.004–0.007 per rival — a tenth of research 05
§1.3's 0.05 — and a slope on its lower bound (in this FAAB league a dropped player's demand does not
rise with his projection; 7 and 11 of the ≈ 180 drops were claimed), within ±2.4 points in both
seasons. Read with care: the predictions then concentrate in 0.9–1.0, so one decile is judged per
season, and the evidence is dropped players in a FAAB league, not a priority league's claims. The
curve is reported, **not** applied to E5: E5 keeps the cold-start curve (said in its output) until
the owner's league's own claim feed exists; whether research 05 §1.3's intercept should change is a
ruling (the numbers are here).

## Decisions (the plan was silent)

- The fitted distribution lives on the cold-start support extended to 14 and 20 points per week;
  linear binning keeps each run's mean; observations above 20 are clamped and counted.
- Prior strengths: the surplus prior is worth 8 runs, the drift prior 40 team-weeks; `league_fitted`
  needs 13 runs; the drift leaves its prior at 30 team-weeks (research 05 §8.4's ~30 floor).
- Acquisition totals bound c from above only; c is never raised to the bound.
- Below 13 runs the whole fit stays cold (the drift alone does not make a premium `league_fitted`).
- C4's "written up" is enforced: a non-reproducing season must carry an explaining finding; a
  coin flip explains a failure only for the reading it hit and only when ESPN's seeds follow that
  chain everywhere above it.
- The replay's held-out protocol fits each season's premium on the other season.
- C5's verdict is read on the Phase 3 forecast (v2-opportunity's week-ahead expected points over
  replacement, held with the P(role holds) prior); the trailing-4 forecast is reported beside it. A
  candidate the C1 excerpt does not hold keeps the trailing-4 forecast; a bye projects his next game
  from the same as-of.
- The demand-curve fit searches q0 in [0.0005, 0.5] and q1 in [0.0005, 1] (the cold start well
  inside) and flags a parameter on its bound.
- The clears calibration uses the next-week re-add as "claimed"; the two-week window is reported.

## Needs time or the live league

- **The owner's league's own feed.** `premium_basis: "league_fitted"` for the owner's league needs
  its claim runs (`mTransactions2` WAIVER / WAIVER_ERROR rows, cookie-authenticated) for c and one
  logged E5 run per processing run (`W` and the best candidate's `s`) for the surplus distribution —
  at least 13 runs, one regular season. Until the E5 tool persists those runs and passes
  `premium_fit`, E5 stays `cold_start_table` (said in its output). The code path is proven here on
  the replay league's feed.
- **P(clears_to_FA) on real claims.** The calibration against realised rival claims (WAIVER rows
  plus the nightly pool snapshot for the unclaimed) accrues during the season; `calibrateClearsToFa`
  is the function the retrospective calls, soft below 30 observations.
- **Seeding on the owner's previous seasons:** Stage C (live reads; the function is the one proven
  on the nine recorded seasons).
