## The replay

- **Held-out seasons:** `league-b`'s recorded **2024 and 2025** seasons
  (`fixtures/espn/recorded/history/<season>/league-b`, keyless and scrubbed; docs/evals/phase3-data.md)
  — the only league-seasons whose box scores carry ESPN's weekly projection beside each rostered
  player's actual, 17 scoring periods each, ten teams, half-PPR. Nothing is fitted on them here:
  every model parameter is a shipped constant. Two of those constants were measured by research 05
  §3.2 on the same NFL seasons from public nflverse data, not on this league — the QB weekly CV
  (2025) and the same-team correlations (2024–2025) — so "held out" covers every choice this replay
  makes, not the research behind those two numbers.
- **Decisions:** every team-week whose roster is complete (a team-week with a withheld entry is
  skipped — the manifest's `incomplete`). Nobody is locked: the decision is taken before any
  kickoff, with game-day information — ESPN's recorded weekly projections on finished seasons are
  game-day values (exactly 0 for every inactive player; docs/evals/phase3-projections.md "ESPN"),
  so the baseline "start by ESPN's projection" and every model read the same availability.
- **Distributions:** E1 as shipped, for every rostered player of the week — ESPN's weekly projection
  is the mean (`weight_espn = 1.0`, ADV OBJ-02). Two bases, one per toolset:
  - **position_cv** — what `EFF_TOOLSET=core` serves (`v1-ensemble`, the positional CV);
  - **v2-opportunity where modelled** — what `EFF_TOOLSET=full` serves since Phase 3: every
    QB/RB/WR/TE v2 can model (a GSIS id, an NFL team, a game that week, a game history) takes
    `v2-opportunity`'s served first-week distribution — the opportunity simulation re-run at ESPN's
    mean and pinned to it — exactly as `src/mcp/tools/analytics.ts` merges it (runV2 → mergeV2);
    anyone else keeps E1's Phase 2 basis (an RB/WR/TE with ≥ 3 trailing games: the volume ×
    efficiency simulation; else position_cv); K and D/ST keep position_cv. v2's request is assembled
    from the committed C1 excerpt the way the store assembles it
    (`tests/domain/analytics/calibration/helpers/v2-history.ts`: this season's games before the week
    with the usage extras, the prior season's without, ffopportunity xTD, team-games before the week,
    the week's market line, opponent and venue weather, the season's regressed matchup table, the
    shipped priors and parameters fitted on 2023 only), with availability at game time (ESPN's
    projection 0 = OUT, the information the ESPN baseline carries). 500 samples per player
    (E2's own call asks for 2 000; the mean is pinned either way — only the quantiles' Monte-Carlo
    noise depends on it).
  v1's trailing lines are the player's own earlier weeks as ESPN recorded them in the same box
  scores (the actual raw stats through the plan 08 translator) — only weeks he was rostered in this
  league, never the week itself or later.
- **Teams:** a history box score lists each player's NFL team at *recording* time (2026), so the
  replay reads the team of each week's game from the actual stat entry (else his nearest earlier,
  then later, game that season). Reading the listed team instead put players on byes they never had,
  zeroed their projections and changed the `mean` lineup in 10 team-weeks (measured once,
  before the fix; `tests/domain/analytics/calibration/history.test.ts` holds the rule).
- **Rules:** E2 under `auto`, `mean` and `pwin` against the opponent's roster; "start by ESPN's
  projection" and "start by last week's points" (plan 10 A11a's baselines — the same exact
  assignment, by those numbers; week 1 has no last week); the hindsight-best legal lineup; the
  manager's lineup as played. Regret = hindsight − realised (≥ 0), per team-week.
- **Head-to-head:** a matchup counts when both rosters are complete and each side's starters sum to
  its total (a two-week final does not). Its `P(win)` is the model's for the two lineups **as
  played** — the matchup's forecast, the event ESPN's `winProbability` forecasts too — against the
  real result (primary); E2's own `p_win_after` (the recommended lineup against the opponent's
  best-by-mean lineup, as E2 assumes) is scored against that counterfactual's result (secondary).
  Each matchup contributes both sides, so deciles k and 9 − k mirror each other.

## C2 — `P(win)` calibration per decile

A decile is checked when it holds ≥ 30 forecasts (research 05 §8.4); the literal criterion is
|realised − forecast| ≤ 5 points in every checked decile. A ±5-point gap is below one standard error
until a decile holds ≈ 90 forecasts at p ≈ ½, and resolving it at 95 % needs ≈ 350–380 — so each
decile also shows its Wilson interval, the forecasts it would need, and a family-wise (Bonferroni)
consistency check. Status `met` = every checked decile within ±5; `unresolved` = a literal miss only
where the sample cannot resolve ±5, every decile consistent; `not_met` = a resolvable miss or an
inconsistent decile.

Reading it: the forecasts cluster near ½ (half-PPR, ten teams, ESPN means — most matchups are close
to even on paper), so deciles 0–2 and 7–9 hold too few matchups to check. Where the deciles are full
the model is close: within a point at 0.4–0.6 on both bases. The miss at 0.3–0.4 / 0.6–0.7 is an
under-confidence on both bases — 5.6 points on 49 matchups (position_cv), 8.4 on 47 (v2) — each
inside the decile's Wilson interval; ±5 cannot be resolved there below ≈ 355 forecasts. The sign is
consistent with C1's finding that, at game time, the served intervals run slightly wide for players
known to be active (the ensemble's p10–p90 covered 81.7 % / 82.1 % against 80 %): a wider spread per
player is a wider spread per lineup, so P(win) leans toward ½. No dispersion is re-fitted here — the
held-out seasons would then not be held out. E2's own `p_win_after` (scored against its
counterfactual, the opponent's best-by-mean lineup) is `met` under v2 (within 4.9 points in every
checked decile) and `unresolved` under position_cv (5.2 on 82); its Brier (0.244) is worse than the

mistakes the forecast calls correctly. (The B2b text blamed the optimiser's selection bias; Stage C
showed that bias cancels in this family — see "E2's own `p_win_after`" below.)
**The noise floor (B2b gate round 3).** The yardstick for the decomposition's reliability term is
what a perfectly calibrated forecaster issuing the same 272 forecasts would score from sampling alone
(`reliability_if_calibrated`, printed in each family's line above; exact formula and a seeded
Monte-Carlo in `tests/domain/analytics/calibration/reliability.test.ts`). The primary family sits
**below** it on both bases — 0.0020 (position_cv) and 0.0031 (v2) against ≈ 0.0060 — so the decile
misses are no larger than calibrated forecasts produce on this sample; a dispersion defect large
enough for ten-team deciles to see would put the term above the floor. E2's own `p_win_after` sits

- **The floor is a mean, not a bound.** A perfectly calibrated forecaster issuing exactly these
  forecasts exceeds 0.0114 / 0.0110 one time in ten (P = 0.104 / 0.115; its 95th percentile is
  0.0144). Mirrored deciles move together and the two sparse tail deciles weigh as much as the full
  central ones, so the term has few degrees of freedom and a spread about as large as its mean:
  "twice the floor" is ordinary. The primary family sits at P = 0.87 / 0.71.
- **The tails are a dozen binary results.** The twelve 0.2–0.3 matchups (the 0.7–0.8 ones seen from
  the other side) carry 0.0089 of the 0.0114 (2 × 12 × 0.318² / 272, from the decile table). By
  their margins the favourite out-scored the underdog by 12.7 points against a forecast 18.4
  (position_cv; within one standard error; 6.9 against 19.2 under v2, 1.8 standard errors), and four
  of the underdog's seven wins were by fewer than ten points. Seven or more underdog wins has a
  binomial tail of 0.02 for that one decile — the worst of four independent decile pairs, and
  family-wise consistent, as the decile table already says.
- **The mechanisms that would make it a defect are absent.** Across all 136 margins the slope is
  1.01 (95 % CI 0.47–1.55): no regression toward 0, which is what a selection bias favouring the
  side with the higher projection, or over-dispersed means, would produce. The standardised
  residual's mean square is 1.041 / 1.007 inside 0.78–1.25: the spread is right.
- **The optimiser's selection bias is real — in level, on both sides.** A best-by-mean lineup
  realises 3.88 points below its projected total per team-week against 2.75 for the lineup as played,
  and the opponent's best-by-mean lineup out-projects his lineup as played by 1.29 points but
  out-scores it by 0.16. E2's counterfactual pits one best-by-mean lineup against another, so the bias
  cancels in the margin (summed over both sides of every matchup the margin's error is exactly 0) and
  leaves `p_win_after` unbiased. The B2b attribution was wrong; this section supersedes it. A
  selection-bias correction or a re-fitted dispersion here would fit noise — and the fixtures carry
  ESPN's weekly projections only for the held-out 2024–2025 seasons, so nothing could be fitted on
  2023 without touching them.

distribution (P ≥ 0.05, below its 95th percentile); slope interval holding 1; the mean square inside
its range; the selection bias larger for a best-by-mean lineup than for the lineup as played, and
cancelling in E2's margin. **Reported, not changed:** a comparison between a best-by-mean lineup and
